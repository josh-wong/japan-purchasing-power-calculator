# Yen Lens

**Product requirements · v1.0**

## Goal

Help foreign visitors understand what share of a monthly income in yen a purchase in Japan takes. Residents can use the same comparison. Apply that share to a comparison income in another currency to show the equivalent cost as the main result. Show exchange-rate conversion separately. Both incomes can be real or hypothetical.

## Core experience

1. Choose a purchase from **Try an example** or enter a price in Japanese yen.
2. Use the example or enter **Income in yen**. Under **Comparison income**, choose the currency and enter **Monthly income** in that currency. Both amounts represent monthly take-home income.
3. Read the equivalent cost under **Same share of income**. Below it, see the **Price at the exchange rate** and what percentage of each income the purchase costs.

The first visit opens with the JP¥5,000 attraction ticket preset, US dollars, and editable example incomes of JP¥250,000 and US$4,000.00 per month. Use one guidance paragraph: “Enter monthly income after taxes and other deductions. The incomes can be real or hypothetical. The prefilled amounts are editable examples, not national averages.” Include “Your inputs stay in this browser and aren’t saved.” in the footer disclaimer. Changing currency carries over the yen fields and restores an amount previously entered in that currency during the visit, if any. Otherwise, prefill the comparison income with the equivalent of US$4,000, rounded up to the nearest thousand, or ten thousand for amounts of at least 100,000. Directly below the **Comparison income** heading, show “Use the example or enter a monthly income in the selected currency.” Associate this guidance with the group and its income input. Keep it visible when editing or switching currency; do not ask for personal income. **Restore defaults** restores the starting price, incomes, and USD currency and clears remembered incomes for other currencies.

Selecting a preset fills the editable price field and recalculates the results. Show “These are example prices. Actual prices vary.” directly below the **Try an example** label and above the dropdown.

| Casual lunch | JP¥1,000 |

Label the main result **Same share of income**. Show an approximate yen equivalent next to the equivalent cost, for example “≈ JP¥12,739 at the ECB rate”, converted from the unrounded equivalent with the exchange-rate snapshot. Hide it when no rate is available, when the equivalent cost is unavailable, or when the comparison currency is yen. Don’t repeat the rate date or the outdated flag there, because **Price at the exchange rate** already shows them. Below the equivalent cost, connect the entered yen price, its share of the entered income in yen, and the equivalent cost: “JP¥5,000 is about 2% of the entered income in yen. The same share of the comparison income is US$80.00.” for the default example. Preserve fractional yen in the explanation when entered. Use “about” for displayed positive percentages of 0.01% or more. Group the percentages under **Share of monthly take-home income**. Label their bases “Yen price ÷ income in yen” and “Converted price ÷ comparison income” so only the comparison percentage implies conversion. In **How it’s calculated**, explain why entering a higher comparison income raises the equivalent cost at the same percentage and how the converted price depends on the exchange rate.

The published app uses dated European Central Bank reference rates, usually refreshed each working day. The mockup requests the latest available ECB rate when opened and includes a dated ECB snapshot for offline use.

## v1.0 scope

- Single-page web app for phones and desktops, in English and Japanese. A language control in the header switches between them. Start in Japanese when the browser's preferred language is Japanese, and in English otherwise. Keep the choice in memory for the current page only, so each page starts again from the browser's language, and keep entered values and results when switching.
- Offer an icon button in the header that cycles through **System**, **Light**, and **Dark**. Default to **System** and remember the chosen theme in the browser.
- Publish the app on GitHub Pages and refresh its exchange-rate snapshot through GitHub Actions. Link to the [source repository](https://github.com/josh-wong/yenlens) from a GitHub icon beside the appearance button.
- One purchase at a time, with a currency picker covering the supported European Central Bank reference-rate currencies other than JPY. JPY is the fixed purchase currency.
- Take-home income means income after tax and payroll deductions. Give the yen purchase price its own row. Within **Monthly take-home income**, put **Income in yen** first, then group **Comparison currency** with **Monthly income** under a **Comparison income** legend. Place the currency picker immediately before the comparison income input in keyboard order. Show the two comparison controls side by side where space permits and stack them on narrow screens.
- Accept numbers with or without grouping commas, including misplaced commas in the whole-number portion and allow a period for decimals in any monetary field. Show “Number format: 4,000.50 or 4000.50.” below the income fields and associate it with all numeric inputs. Show formatting and validation errors when the visitor leaves a field, and clear that field’s error when editing resumes. Continue calculating valid inputs while typing. Ignore grouping commas in the whole-number portion when calculating, and regroup valid amounts when the visitor leaves the field, such as `2500,000` becoming `2,500,000`. A period remains the decimal separator. Calculate while the visitor types and normalize valid entries when they leave the field.
- Lead with the equivalent cost. If an income is blank, prompt for it in the main result and show the converted price below when the price and exchange rate are available. Offer a short explanation of the math and a reset to the examples.
- Display familiar currency symbols and the standard number of decimal places for each currency. Use US$80.00 for USD and JP¥5,000 for JPY. Keep currency codes in the picker and field labels.
- Keep financial inputs in the browser session. The calculation requires no account or personal data collection.
- Offer opt-in usage analytics through Google Analytics so the site owner can see visit counts, pages viewed, and rough traffic sources. Ask every visitor before collecting anything, with **Allow analytics** and **Decline** given equal weight, and keep the calculator fully usable without a choice. Analytics never receives entered amounts, chosen currencies or presets, or results. Visitors can change their choice at any time from **Analytics settings** in the footer, and a [privacy notice](../../privacy.html) explains what is collected and why.

A future version could add sourced income profiles, regional comparisons, or historical affordability. v1.0 models the amounts the visitor enters.

## Acceptance criteria

- **System** follows the device’s appearance, including changes while the page is open. **Light** and **Dark** override it. Reloading restores the theme when browser storage is available. Browser storage holds only the theme and the analytics choice, never calculator inputs or the language choice.
- The first result is the equivalent cost under **Same share of income**. The default inputs produce US$80.00, with a yen equivalent beside it when the dated example rate is available. Below it, the dated example rate produces a converted price of US$31.40. The income percentages are 2% for the income in yen and 0.79% for the comparison income.
- Selecting a preset fills only the price. A manually entered price that differs from the preset selects the hidden, disabled **Custom price** option. Reset restores the JP¥5,000 attraction ticket and the starting scenario.
- The converted price remains available with both income fields blank. Each income percentage appears when its inputs are available. Switching currency restores the income entered for that currency. Comparison-income guidance stays below the group heading.
- Percentages above 100% are displayed in full. A zero purchase price is valid. Income amounts must be positive.
- If an exchange rate is unavailable, show that status for the converted price and omit the yen equivalent next to the main result. Calculate the percentage of income in yen and the equivalent cost from the entered amounts. Each available conversion displays its source and rate date. Show the numeric currency pair directly below the monetary amount in **Price at the exchange rate**, before the source and date. Label it “Exchange rate used” and omit the trailing period.
- All monetary fields accept input such as `5,000.50`. Preserve fractional input when it affects the calculation. Calculated monetary results use the currency's standard precision, including two places for USD and whole units for JPY and KRW.
- The form and its results work at a 320 CSS-pixel viewport, with keyboard navigation, visible focus, and screen-reader labels for the fields.
- Without a choice, the analytics panel appears and the site makes no request to Google and sets no analytics cookie. **Allow analytics** starts collection; **Decline** collects nothing. The choice is remembered for 12 months when browser storage is available, and for the current visit otherwise.
- **Analytics settings** reopens the panel and shows the current choice. Turning analytics off stops collection immediately and deletes the analytics cookies.
- A Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal counts as a decline. The panel doesn’t appear, and **Analytics settings** explains why analytics is off.
- The calculator, the consent panel, and the privacy notice appear in Japanese when the browser prefers Japanese, and the header language control switches every visible string, number, and date format without clearing inputs, results, or errors. The `lang` attribute follows the selected language. The language choice isn't stored, added to the address, or sent to analytics, and analytics receives the English page title.
- No analytics request, page address, or stored value contains a price, income, currency, preset, or result. Builds without a measurement ID show no analytics panel or settings control.
